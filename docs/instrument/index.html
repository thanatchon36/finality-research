01 — target selection

Why crypto beats the bank

An attacker is a rational investor: expected payoff = P(breach)·Value·(1 − P(recovery))·(1 − laundering haircut) − P(caught)·penalty − cost. Pick a venue, move its dials, and watch the ranking reorder. The decisive term is recovery — transaction finality.

02 — systemic loss

When the banking core breaks

A loss-distribution model: attacks arrive (Poisson), each causes a loss (log-normal body + heavy Pareto tail), and the largest can cascade through interbank exposure. Monte-Carlo gives the annual-loss distribution, a Cyber-VaR, Expected Shortfall and — the honest form of “when” — a return period.

03 — extreme values

Reading a tail nobody has seen

Above a high enough threshold u, exceedances of almost any loss distribution follow a Generalized Pareto Distribution (the Pickands–Balkema–de Haan theorem). Fit one to the incidents in your record, extrapolate to the 1-in-1,000-year loss, and check it against the full simulation, which plays the truth. 04 — contagion

How one breach becomes a system event

Banks hold claims on each other, so an equity loss at one bank marks down the assets of every bank that lent to it, and so on round after round (linear DebtRank). Hit one bank, or a shared vendor that many banks depend on, and watch the loss travel. Once the spectral radius ρ(Λ) of the exposure matrix passes 1, shocks stop dying out.

Scenario

A cyber event wipes out part of the equity of the bank or banks it hits. Exposure counts each bank’s claims on other banks as a multiple of its own equity. Tap a bank in the network to hit it.
